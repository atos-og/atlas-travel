# Atlas — Brand & Product Brief

**A plain-language foundation for the visual identity.**

Updated September 24, 2026. The owner approved the supplied Atlas identity board: the A-shaped route symbol, Atlas wordmark, Midnight/Mint/Sand palette, and the shown light, dark, and avatar directions. See [the approved visual identity](VISUAL_IDENTITY.md). Taglines and unspecified production details remain proposals; an editable font or vector master has not been supplied.

## 1. Atlas in one sentence

**Atlas is a personal travel assistant that helps people find, compare, and plan trips through a conversation.**

It starts with a familiar question: “Where would you like to go?” It then helps turn a vague intention into a smaller, clearer set of choices.

The long-term vision is one helpful travel companion, from choosing how to get there to deciding what to do after arriving.

## 2. Why Atlas exists

Planning a trip often means opening many pages, comparing prices that are difficult to understand, checking dates repeatedly, and trying to decide whether saving money is worth a longer journey.

People do not always know the exact airport, the best travel day, or which option fits their needs. They may know only that they want to visit Colombia, spend less, avoid connections, or make good use of a few days away.

Atlas should make that process feel manageable. It asks useful questions, presents a few understandable options, and helps the traveler adjust the plan.

**The desired feeling is: “I understand my options, and I know what to do next.”**

## 3. Who it is for

The initial experience is designed for Portuguese-speaking travelers who are comfortable using WhatsApp. Brazil and travel within Latin America are natural starting contexts, but the identity should be suitable for a broader travel audience.

The intended audience includes:

- People who want to compare trips without spending hours searching.
- Budget-conscious travelers who still care about time and convenience.
- Occasional travelers who appreciate guidance and clear explanations.
- People who begin with an idea rather than a fully specified itinerary.
- Travelers who prefer a conversation to filling out a long form.

These are intended audiences, not findings from completed customer research. Atlas is currently being used privately while the experience is developed and tested.

## 4. The promise

**Make travel choices clearer, so planning feels easier.**

Atlas should help a person understand the trade-offs between cost, time, and the kind of journey they want. The brand should communicate useful guidance, honest information, and freedom to explore.

The promise is clarity and assistance. It is not a guarantee of the cheapest fare in the world, complete coverage of every travel company, or a perfect trip with no uncertainty.

## 5. What travelers can do today

The current private version helps a traveler:

1. Start a conversation on WhatsApp.
2. Choose departure and destination airports, with clarification when a place has several possibilities.
3. Give departure and return dates, including supported phrases such as “tomorrow” or “October 23 this year” in Portuguese.
4. Choose the number of adults and a preference such as lower price, shorter travel time, or no stops.
5. Set a total ticket budget, or continue without a limit.
6. Confirm the trip details before the search.
7. Compare up to four returned flight options.
8. Select an offer and open it in Google Flights through a WhatsApp button.
9. Adjust dates, passengers, preferences, or budget and continue the conversation.

The current flight experience covers economy round trips for adults. The budget refers to tickets for all included adults in both directions, not a complete holiday budget. Prices and availability must still be checked with the provider. Atlas does not currently sell or issue tickets.

Supported natural phrases make the conversation easier, but Atlas does not yet understand every possible request. If something is unclear, it should ask rather than guess.

## 6. Where the product is going

The identity should leave room for these planned capabilities:

| Direction | Traveler benefit | Current position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flights and buses in one comparison | Choose the transport that fits the journey | Flights available experimentally; buses planned |
| Budget-led travel | Find options that respect what the person wants to spend | Total flight-price limit available; destination discovery planned |
| Flexible dates | See whether another day improves the trip | Planned |
| Personalized sightseeing plans | Know what to do, in an order that makes sense | Planned |
| Remembered preferences | Avoid repeating the same choices each time | Planned |
| Useful travel alerts | Learn when a relevant option changes | Planned |
| Contextual suggestions | Discover what Atlas can help with next | Basic help available; richer suggestions planned |

For example, a future conversation might move from selecting flights to planning three days of sightseeing. If the journey is too expensive, Atlas could suggest different dates or another way to travel.

These are intentions for the product, not features to advertise as available today. They should guide a flexible identity rather than a list of launch promises.

## 7. What should make Atlas distinctive

Atlas is intended to connect the journey's decisions: how to travel, how much to spend, and how to enjoy the destination.

Its character comes from a combination of:

- **Conversation:** a familiar, approachable way to start.
- **Choice:** a small set of understandable alternatives.
- **Personal relevance:** options shaped by the traveler's priorities.
- **Honesty:** clear limits, current information, and no invented certainty.
- **Continuity:** a future path from finding transport to planning the visit.

Atlas should feel broader than a fare-alert service or a discount feed. It should not feel like an airline, an official WhatsApp service, or a conventional travel agency promising to handle every booking.

No claim that these ideas are exclusive to Atlas has been established. The identity should express its own purpose without imitating another travel assistant.

## 8. The meaning of the name

An atlas helps people understand places and the relationships between them. It suggests orientation, discovery, perspective, and the possibility of going somewhere new.

For this brand, the strongest interpretation is **a guide to possibilities**.

The name does not require a literal map, a globe, or a mythological figure carrying the world. Those may be explored, but the mark should remain simple enough to recognize inside a small circular profile photo.

Keep **Atlas** as the public-facing name. “Travel assistant” can act as a descriptor when context is needed.

## 9. Personality and voice

Atlas should be:

| Quality | How it should feel |
| --- | --- |
| Clear | Easy to understand at a glance |
| Thoughtful | Asks questions that improve the trip |
| Calm | Reduces pressure instead of adding urgency |
| Capable | Organized, consistent, and attentive |
| Curious | Invites discovery without overwhelming the traveler |
| Approachable | Friendly without becoming childish |
| Honest | Explains uncertainty and limitations plainly |

The voice should be warm, direct, and useful. Short sentences are usually better than elaborate travel slogans. Avoid pressure, exaggerated enthusiasm, artificial scarcity, and claims of perfect knowledge.

Illustrative English voice examples:

- “Where would you like to go?”
- “What matters most: price, travel time, or fewer stops?”
- “Is that your total budget for everyone traveling?”
- “These options are above your budget. Would you like to change the dates or the limit?”
- “Check the final price and baggage details before booking.”

Future-only example: “Would you like help planning what to do when you arrive?” Do not use this as an available offer until sightseeing plans are supported.

The brief and brand materials use English. The current traveler conversation uses Portuguese; these English examples communicate tone rather than promise English-language conversation support.

## 10. Approved visual direction

The approved mark combines a rounded A silhouette, a winding route in negative space, and a mint circular accent. The wordmark is bold and rounded. Use the supplied artwork as the visual source of truth; do not substitute a plane, compass, map, robot, or newly generated interpretation.

The light composition places the Midnight symbol and wordmark on Sand. The dark composition reverses the main artwork to a light color on Midnight, retaining Mint. The avatar uses the standalone light symbol and Mint accent inside a dark circle.

## 11. Approved color and lettering approach

| Color | Approved value | Intended role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Midnight | `#0B1F3B` | Main artwork, strong text, dark backgrounds |
| Mint | `#2EE6B6` | Distinctive circular accent and restrained highlights |
| Sand | `#FAF8F3` | Light backgrounds and reversed artwork |

This palette replaces the earlier navy/teal/ivory/coral proposal. Color labels in the supplied board define the values; shading in the raster artwork is not an additional color specification. Use Midnight for text on light surfaces and a light foreground on Midnight. Keep Mint primarily as an accent and verify text contrast before using it for labels.

Preserve the supplied wordmark rather than guessing its font. The reference does not identify a font family or provide editable lettering. Supporting typography remains to be selected and should be readable and visually compatible.

## 12. WhatsApp profile image

This is the first-priority asset. It must work when seen very small and cropped into a circle.

Approved avatar direction and production guidance:

- One recognizable Atlas symbol, centered.
- A solid background with strong contrast.
- Generous space around the mark so circular cropping does not cut it.
- No tagline, tiny destination names, detailed map, or crowded illustration.
- Prefer the standalone symbol to the full wordmark when the name becomes unreadable.

Create a square master, for example 1024 × 1024, and inspect circular previews at several small sizes, including 32 and 64 pixels. These are design working sizes, not a claim about WhatsApp's required upload dimensions.

Deliver both light and dark versions and a one-color version. The symbol should remain recognizable without gradients or animation.

## 13. Cover and wider brand compositions

Prepare a flexible horizontal composition for a potential cover, project page, or social banner. Confirm the destination surface before making a final crop; this brief does not assume that every WhatsApp account has the same cover feature.

Suggested composition:

- Atlas wordmark and a short descriptor in a clear focal area.
- One supporting visual: an abstract route, a horizon, or quiet map-inspired lines.
- Plenty of open space rather than a collage of landmarks.
- Important content near the center so alternate crops remain usable.
- No fare prices or unsupported feature claims in permanent brand artwork.

A 1600 × 900 working composition can establish the visual direction, followed by a wider adaptation for the actual destination. Do not treat either as a platform requirement.

Suggested cover copy:

**Atlas**

*Your next journey, made clear.*

*A personal travel assistant.*

Keep the tagline optional. The name and symbol should work without it.

## 14. Draft positioning copy

**Primary tagline proposal:** Your next journey, made clear.

**Alternative:** A clearer way to travel.

**Short description:** Atlas helps you compare travel options and take the next step through a simple conversation.

**Current-product description:** Explore round-trip flight options, compare prices and travel times, set your ticket budget, and open an offer to check it with the provider.

**Long-term vision statement:** From choosing how to get there to planning what to do, Atlas aims to make travel feel easier to organize and more personal.

These are draft creative lines. They have not undergone naming, trademark, or audience testing. Avoid claims such as “every fare,” “always the cheapest,” “book everything here,” or “your entire trip is ready” while the product cannot substantiate them.

## 16. How to judge the identity

A successful direction should answer yes to these questions:

- Can the profile symbol be recognized at a small size?
- Does it feel trustworthy, helpful, and welcoming?
- Does it suggest travel without restricting Atlas to flights alone?
- Can it work in one color and on both light and dark backgrounds?
- Is it clearly Atlas rather than a variation of a familiar travel brand?
- Can the identity support future sightseeing and bus features naturally?
- Does the wording describe the current product honestly?

The immediate goal is not to make Atlas look enormous or fully finished. It is to give a useful, growing travel product an identity people can recognize and feel comfortable approaching.
